# Product

## Register

product

## Users

SF State undergraduates planning the rest of their degree, most often right after opening their Degree Progress
Report (DPR) in Student Center and before registration. Many are transfer or international (F-1) students; few know
what an MCP server is or have an AI app of their own. They come with one job: "what's left, and when do I take it?",
and they check the answer against their DPR and an advisor.

## Product Purpose

GatorPath reads the student's DPR, checks a semester-by-semester plan against the SF State Bulletin, and builds a
clash-free schedule for next term. The chat is the product: the student asks, and the plan beside it changes; the
page shows the result. Success is classmates actually using it and reporting where it's wrong, and every plan passing
GatorPath's checks. The DPR and an academic advisor are always the official sources.

## Brand Personality

Calm, clear, trustworthy. A careful advisor, not a salesperson: quiet visuals, confident and plain wording ("Checked
against the Bulletin", not "check_plan"), nothing flashy. It says what it knows, flags what it can't check, and sends
the rest to an advisor in Navigator.

## Anti-references

- Notion or Linear-style workspaces: sidebars, boards and grids (rejected 2026-10-31 as complicated, hard to navigate).
- The generic AI SaaS look: gradient heroes, big stat cards, identical card grids, purple-blue glow.
- Student Center / PeopleSoft: cramped tables, tiny text, hard navigation.
- Cartoonish or gamified: badges, mascots, confetti. Degree decisions are serious.

## Design Principles

- Guided, not a workspace: one clear next step on each screen (upload, check, goals, plan, schedule).
- Plain words over system words: say what a requirement means for the student, never a tool or field name.
- Show the source: the DPR, the Bulletin page, or the check that decided it; never hide a failed check.
- The chat drives, the page shows: anything can be changed from the chat, and the page reflects it at once.
- Honest limits: what GatorPath can't verify is said once, plainly, with where to ask.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.2 AA: 4.5:1 text contrast, full keyboard use with visible focus, screen-reader labels on controls and
progress, reduced-motion alternatives. Laptop first, but every screen must work on a phone. Many users read English
as a second language: short sentences, no idioms.
